# Yasis Breath Timer

A breathing pacer for labour preparation — 4 seconds in, 8 seconds out.

**→ [Open the timer](https://attitudesmuff.github.io/pregnancy-48-breath-timer)**

Works on any phone or computer, no install needed. On iPhone, tap Share → Add to Home Screen to use it as a standalone app.

## What it does

A rising tone guides you through a 4-second inhale, then a falling tone through an 8-second exhale — five breaths per minute, the pace most supported by evidence for activating the parasympathetic nervous system.

The phrase sweeps across the screen in sync with the breath, so you read it as one continuous line rather than word by word.

## Features

- **Gliding audio** — continuous rising/falling tone instead of per-second beeps, so the breath stays smooth
- **Guided phrases** — body-scan cues, wave imagery, affirmations, or simple counting
- **English and Dutch** — all phrase sets available in both languages
- **Sound modes** — tone only, tone with turn markers, or tone with per-second counts for learning
- **Vibrate on turn** — optional haptic feedback for eyes-closed use
- **Works offline** — single self-contained HTML file, no server, no dependencies

## The phrases

| Set | Inhale (4s) | Exhale (8s) |
|-----|------------|-------------|
| Softening | I'm filling, easy and slow | jaw loose, shoulders heavy, belly warm and soft, letting everything go |
| The wave | Here it comes, the next wave, rising | and it moves through me and it softens and it slowly rolls away |
| Working | This is my body working | strong and steady, doing exactly what it knows how to do |
| Closer | One more wave, and we are closer | every breath brings you a little further down and into my arms |

All sets also available in Dutch.

## Why these choices

- **Long exhale ratio (1:2)** — extended exhalation shifts cardiac activity toward parasympathetic predominance and increases heart rate variability
- **Continuous tone, not beats** — discrete per-second ticks cause segmented breathing; research uses continuously pitched tones for entrainment
- **Descriptive phrases, not promises** — affirmations that describe what's happening land better than grand claims, which can backfire for people under stress
- **Open vowels at phrase endings** — *gaan*, *weg*, *away*, *arms* — so the breath trails out instead of clipping

## A note

Slow, not big. The clock is long; the breath stays easy. Forcing it deep causes hyperventilation — tingling, dizziness, lightheadedness. The pacer is a practice tool. During actual labour, follow the contraction, not the clock.
